01 · Overview

Designed for the cook who
already knows how to cook.

A self-directed iOS concept. I designed two surfaces on top of a design system: Recipe Detail, where a cook decides whether to trust the recipe, and Cook Mode, where they use it. It's mid-fidelity on purpose, so the focus is on the decisions.

What it is
Curated search for authentic Asian recipes
Only recipes from culturally credible, vetted sources surface in results. For example, Korean Bapsang shows up and Allrecipes doesn't.
Who it's for
Cooks who already cook
Experienced home cooks who get disappointing results with dishes outside their own background, and suspect the recipe is the problem.

02 · The Problem

Finding a recipe is easy.
Trusting it is hard.

You can find a Kimchi Jjigae recipe in seconds. Maangchi has millions of fans. Cultural authorship is visible. But a good cook still can't tell, before buying ingredients, whether a recipe will taste right.

How it works today

Today: find a recipe and hope.

  • ×
    47 results that all sound right. No signal for which will taste like the dish you're trying to make. Sunset Magazine
  • ×
    Search rewards SEO over flavor. "Random ideas hoping to land on page one." Keywords outrank cooking knowledge. Madeleine Kitchen
  • ×
    Even trusted sources fail. Tried Maangchi, Seonkyoung Longest, Aaron & Claire. Still missed. "Maybe it's me." r/KoreanFood · 1 year, unanswered
  • ×
    Reading mode ≠ cooking mode. No app separates the experience of browsing a recipe from the experience of cooking it. Messy hands, glancing from across a kitchen.
How it should work

With Umami: find a recipe you can trust.

  • ◆
    Curated sources only. Korean Bapsang shows up and Allrecipes doesn't. Curation decides what appears. Umami's curation model
  • ◆
    Clear attribution. Every recipe shows who wrote it and why they're worth trusting, above the fold.
  • ◆
    Cook Mode is a different surface. Full-screen modal. One step at a time. Large type, screen stays awake, per-step timers. Designed for messy hands and distance. Recipe Detail → Cook Mode
  • ◆
    Sign-in only when needed. Browse and cook without an account. You only sign in when you want to save. Just-in-time auth

What the research found

Three signals from secondary research (no user interviews yet): forums, cooking blogs, and a Reddit thread that went unanswered for a year.

r/KoreanFood · Secondary research · H1 + H2 + H3

"I've tried Maangchi's recipe, Seonkyoung Longest's, and Aaron & Claire on YouTube. My wife thinks they're just ok. Maybe it's me."

He used popular, culturally authentic sources and still couldn't get it right, so he blamed himself. The thread got one unhelpful reply in a year. This post sums up why I designed Umami: people need someone to vet the sources for them.

Sunset Magazine + Madeleine Kitchen · Secondary research · H2

"Many sites are run by people posting random ideas, hoping to land on page one of a search engine. Throwing keywords into a recipe doesn't guarantee flavour or accuracy."

SEO rewards volume, so search often shows weaker recipes first. A curated layer on top of search addresses that.

Discuss Cooking forum + Plan to Eat blog · Secondary research · H3

"I get tired of sites that parrot what the other cooking blogs say. I'm looking for a bit more authenticity. Any ideas?"

Experienced cooks already solve this by collecting trusted sources over years. Umami gives everyone that list from day one.

04 · Recipe Detail

The recipe as an
editorial object.

Recipe Detail is where a cook decides whether to make the dish. It needs to build trust quickly and stay out of the way.

Direction Exploration

The main layout question was where to put trust signals: in the body text, in a separate block, or as attribution above the fold. My annotation sums up the choice: "attribution = trust, not followers."

Recipe Detail · direction exploration · Wireframe with annotations · trust-first layout selected · Attribution above the fold
1

3

Hero, attribution, and a translucent nav.

The first screen answers two questions: "Is this the right dish?" and "Should I trust this recipe?" The hero image answers the first, and attribution answers the second.

1
Translucent nav, transparent over hero. The nav bar is invisible over the full-bleed hero image. On scroll, it collapses to a solid translucent bar with the recipe title, following standard iOS behavior.
2
Clear attribution. "Adapted from Hyosun, Korean Bapsang" is displayed prominently, above the fold. Cooks who know the source can trust it right away. Cooks who don't know it have somewhere to look.
3
Time, yield, and a single CTA above the fold. 15 min active / 35 min total, serves 4, and one clear action: Cook Mode. A cook can tell if it works for tonight without scrolling.

Ingredients that respond to how you cook.

Two controls most recipe apps lack: scaling and unit conversion. They decide whether someone can actually use the recipe.

1
Serves stepper with live rescaling. Tap + or − and every ingredient quantity updates instantly. Cooking for 2 instead of 4 means half the pork belly, half the kimchi.
2
US / Metric toggle. A persistent toggle at the top of the ingredients section. The preference is remembered.
3
AI ingredient substitution. Swappable ingredients show an indicator. Tapping opens a swap panel with options ranked by taste impact, with a note on how each swap changes the dish. Reverting restores the original.

Substitutions ranked by taste impact.

Most substitution features answer "what can I use instead?" Umami also shows how much each swap changes the dish.

1
Bottom sheet presentation. Follows iOS presentation grammar for focused, dismissible tasks. The sheet appears over the recipe without replacing it, so the cook can see context while making a swap decision.
2
Taste impact scale. Each substitute is ranked 1–3 dots: how much does this swap move the dish from authentic? Swapping miso paste for doenjang is a big change, and the cook should know that.
3
Revert to original. Any swap can be undone. The original ingredient can always be restored.

Direction Exploration

Three Cook Mode layouts evaluated: light surface (legible at desk, high glare at counter), dark surface (high contrast, kitchen-readable from distance), and peek-ahead (shows the next step before you need it). I chose dark because it's easier to read at arm's length in a bright kitchen.

Key Cook Mode Decisions
Decision 01
A full-screen modal

Cook Mode is a full-screen modal cover with a single ✕ to close. That follows iOS conventions for a separate mode, and makes leaving a deliberate action.

→ Full-screen cover (UIModalPresentationFullScreen). ✕ to dismiss.
Decision 02
Confirm-on-exit with real resume

Closing Cook Mode past step 1 triggers an iOS action sheet: Save & Exit (keeps your place in state) or Discard Progress (destructive, shown in red per iOS convention). Returning to the recipe later offers Resume at Step N or Cook Again. Progress is saved, because cooks get interrupted.

→ Action sheet (UIAlertControllerStyleActionSheet). Destructive action in red, secondary outlined.
Decision 03
Dark surface for kitchen ergonomics

Cook Mode is dark because kitchens are bright. A white screen causes glare at arm's length. Dark, high-contrast, large type is readable from across the counter, and the change in look also signals a different mode.

→ bg: #1c1108 (warm near-black). Type: var(--cream) at 28–36pt for step text.
Decision 04
A completion screen before the rating

Finishing the last step yields a dish complete screen first. Then it offers Save, a 5-star rating, and Share. That's the natural time to ask for a rating.

→ Completion state → rating + save CTA. No forced review prompt mid-session.

Applied Decisions
Correct presentation grammar
Bottom sheets for focused tasks (ingredient swaps, sign-in). A full-screen modal for Cook Mode. Action sheets to confirm destructive actions. Each one matches how iOS uses it.
UISheet · UIModalPresentationFullScreen · UIAlertControllerStyleActionSheet
Translucent nav with scroll collapse
Nav bar is transparent over the full-bleed recipe hero image. On scroll, it collapses to solid translucent with the recipe title, following the standard iOS large-title pattern.
UINavigationBar · transparent → blur on scroll
Destructive actions in red
"Discard Progress" in the stop-cooking action sheet uses red, per iOS convention for irreversible actions. A cook 20 minutes into step 4 should notice it before tapping.
.destructive role · UIAlertAction
Screen stay-awake during active cooking
Cook Mode disables the idle timer while a step timer is running. The screen stays on, so a cook with floury hands doesn't have to unlock their phone mid-simmer.
UIApplication.shared.isIdleTimerDisabled = true
07 · What's Next

What's next:
testing and hi-fi.

The prototype tests structure and interactions before hi-fi work begins.

01
Hi-fi execution in Figma. The design system is built. Next, I'll apply the full Craft Cookbook visual language (Brick × Wheat palette, texture, illustration) to Recipe Detail and Cook Mode, then expanding to search and onboarding.
02
Validate the trust signals before committing to hi-fi. Attribution above the fold and late sign-in are untested assumptions. I want to know whether attribution reads as a trust signal before a cook scrolls, and whether curated sources need more explanation. The answers could change the Recipe Detail layout.
03
Motion and handoff spec. Cook Mode modal entry, timer-fired state, and dish completion need motion specs before handoff. Then component annotations and token mapping, so a developer can build from the file on their own.
